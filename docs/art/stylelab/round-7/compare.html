Your shield · the Canopy

A roof over the core, open at the sides

A glass vault on tree-shaped columns, 40 m high at the rim and 58 m at the top, over Station Square, Main Street, Fountain Cross, the Market Hall and the middle of the Promenade. You walk in and out anywhere, because it is a roof, not a box. Its panels slide open on fine days. The locals call it "the Sail", because its lattice was copied from the Albatross's envelope.

From the air, in the rain

Dry inside, weather outside

Rain falls on the whole bowl but stops on the glass. The ground outside is dark and wet, and the core under the vault stays dry.

  • Under the Canopy, weather never closes the outdoor tables, so trade is steady.
  • Outside it (the lake, the homes, the meadow, the Wreck, the hill) weather counts in full.
  • Choosing a pitch becomes a trade-off: steady and dear under the glass, or cheaper and weather-dependent outside.

What it does in /sim (proposed). The town keeps a soil stock. The more leaves and kitchen scraps go to compost, the more the greenhouses grow next season, and greenhouse ingredients get a little cheaper. It is small on purpose: a reason to care, never a chore.

Storm · Main Street, under the glass

The lightning hits the roof, the café stays open

The bolt strikes a rod on the vault and runs across the lattice. Rain beats on the glass above, while the street below stays dry: no umbrellas, and the café's table is still out. A board says "storm today, canopy shut, tables open".

  • Drones are still grounded in a storm; three wait by the flower shop.
  • This replaces round 6's wet Main Street. That wet look now belongs to the streets outside the Canopy.
Four seasons

One promenade, four seasons

The same view in all four: from the Garden Promenade, under the Canopy, out past its rim to Greenhouse Hill and the dome. Each season has 7 game days (about 4.7 hours of play), its own weather mix, its own demand and its own festival banner. Seasons change the look and the demand, never the paths.

Three names for the town

The game's title (O1) stays open. The town name can match it or not.

proposed

Seedfall

The seeds that fell from the sky. Short and easy to say in many languages, and it hints at autumn and growing.

Albatross Landing

Names the ship; "the Landing" for short. Warm, but long.

Kettlebay

The town sits in a bowl (a kettle) by a bay, and it is a cooking town. Playful, but it says less about the story.

The Wreck · you said "try something"

Three tries for the west edge

Same valley, same sunset, same night market inside; only the ship changes. The camera stands further back than in round 6, so you can compare whole silhouettes. Each one ends the world in the west and becomes a stage 5 place.

proposed

A. The Albatross, Ark Flight 9

The airship from round 6, now with its story: "Ark Flight 9" over the lit cargo hatch and ramp, right of the market, down which the animals walked out, and the glowing seed library ports on the tail piece. At the foot of the ramp (too small to see here) are a plaque, crates and a few hens that never left. Fewer rust panels, and they now lie on the hull.

  • Best fit: an airship comes down softly enough for people and animals to walk away, and it explains the Wildbelt and Greenhouse Hill.

B. The Pelican, a freight plane

Your original idea. A 2100s heavy freight plane lying tilted on the slope, broken behind the wing. Its cargo nose is hinged up, the broken near wing points at the sky, and the far wing rests up the hillside. The market is behind the side cargo door.

  • Good fit: the story stays the same, with a lucky belly landing on the meadow, and the cargo door becomes the ramp.

C. The Stella, a starliner

The most retro-future of the three: a 1950s-style rocket liner on its side, nose in the hill, fins in the air, rows of lit portholes and an airstair into the market.

  • Weakest story fit: a rocket carrying cows needs a bigger tale (flights home from the Moon farms), and it is less cosy.
Player homes

A flat for every player, for free

Basics are free in 2222, so each player gets a flat in the Crew House: 16 small flats by the Promenade, with a shared kitchen table and a roof garden.

  • Decorate it with furniture, plants and posters bought with credits.
  • A wardrobe to change your look, and a mailbox for fan letters, rival challenges and the town paper.
  • A bed that ends your day. Proposed: the night only skips when every player is in bed.
  • Your door is private by default. You can open it to friends or invite one player.
  • Flats are part of the host's save. Later, with credits, you can move to a house of your own.
