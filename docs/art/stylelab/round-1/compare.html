Worktown Style Lab

The town is the future as people dreamed it in the 1930s–70s, finally built after the dark era, with plants where the farms used to be. This round tries three shape languages and two palettes on the same objects, in the same light.

Everything here is modelled by script in Blender (no downloads) and rendered with light close to what the game engine can show. Click any picture to see it large. At the bottom you can put your pick together and copy it into the chat.

Knobs and handles

  1. K1Small round chrome knob, as on the pastel kitchen cabinets. Neat, but small for older hands and eyes.
  2. K2Big ribbed dial with a pointer. Reads from across the room and shows its setting; the natural stove knob.
  3. K3Chrome D-handle. Friendly, easy to spot, very 1950s.
  4. K4Long bar handle. Calm and modern; used on the oven doors.
  5. K5Chrome lever with a ball end. The "strange future" one: fun for special machines.

One variant through the day

S3 P1 (my starting bet) at noon, sunset and night. The question here is whether the style holds up in every light, not which light is nicest.

Can everyone read it?

The same S3 P1 noon shots without colour (as someone with weak contrast vision would see the differences) and with the two common kinds of red-green colour blindness.

What I noticed

The soft kitchen works

The pill cabinets, the patchwork floor and the orange stove read as one friendly place. This is the strongest interior.

Streamline gives the town character

The ribbed sign tower, the curved corners and the rocket car with its grille "face" make the street feel like a place with a story. The soft houses look pleasant but ordinary.

Sunset is the mood picture

The pastel haze with lit signs is the closest to the poster you liked, and it makes even the soft shapes look special.

P1 has too little contrast

Without colour, the mint cabinets, the mustard doors and the sage wall have almost the same lightness and merge. For older players round 2 should darken the cabinets or lighten the wall.

Colour alone is not enough

With red-green colour blindness the orange stove and the mustard doors turn the same yellow, and the tomato loses its red. Shape has to carry the meaning (the ribbed dial, the tomato's leaf star), with colour as a bonus.

Weak spots for round 2

The soft car looks like a tub with a cushion. The people are friendly but stiff: the apron is a flat board and the bodies are tubes. Night is moody, but darker than a game can be.
