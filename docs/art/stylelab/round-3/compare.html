Later · services

Kept up, still lived-in

Their own sign, a delivery van, a queue, a "best hash in town" clipping in the window. Lighter grit, but a dented bin and cracks stay: success is not sterile.

How much grit?

Grit is one setting, so every model in the game follows it. It drives matte surfaces, small colour differences between objects, and grime in corners and at the foot of walls. In the game this is baked shading plus one shared dirt texture, which is cheap.

The dirt layer during service

Same kitchen, same grit. On the right, mid-rush: sauce on the tiles, flour and stains on the worktop, stacks of dishes, boxes and a crate on the floor, order tickets taped up. Cleaning is already gameplay, so this layer appears during service and goes away when players clean.

People on a working day

No more identical smiles. Each face gets an expression and a small asymmetry; aprons pick up stains; hair gets untidy. A new helper joins with a sturdier build and rolled-up trousers.

My review of round 3

Not marketing any more

The stage 1 and stage 2 pictures feel like real places with a history. The mess carries the story without words.

Roughness has a job in the game

Wear shows the climb, the dirt layer is the cleaning gameplay, and rivals can wear their character (a spotless chain, a cosy family place).

Faces are human now

Mixed expressions remove the creepy sameness. The system is cheap: brows, lids and mouth by expression.

Still rough edges in the lab itself

Rough grit can look blotchy on big floors. Stray hair and stains are simple shapes. The van's side lettering and the cart's cash tin lid are placeholders.

For the game

Mess pieces must become decals and a small prop kit, and the dirt layer needs its own spec (when it appears, how cleaning removes it). The handwriting font here is a Windows font; the game needs a licensed one.
