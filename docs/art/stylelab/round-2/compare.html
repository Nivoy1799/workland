Worktown Style Lab 2

Round 1 compared styles with quick models. This round shows how good the models can get: people built from a proper body generator, three hero models at full detail, and the same street dressed by three different restaurant themes.

People

Bodies now grow from a stick skeleton, which gives shoulders, a waist, elbows, knees and posture, and each body is ready for a rig. Clothes are real layers: a tucked shirt with rolled sleeves, a cardigan, a vest, a skirt, an apron that wraps and ties. Faces have eyes with a highlight, a nose, ears and brows. Older people stand a little bent, wear glasses and have softer eyes.

Hero models, soft and streamline

Each model in both shape languages, built with the care I would put into a shipped model. Left is S1 soft, right is S2 streamline.

Personalisation: one grammar, many dialects

Fixed for everyone

  • Shape language, bevels, proportions
  • Light at every time of day
  • Palette structure: a base family plus one accent
  • The building shell and its sizes

Free per business

  • A theme colour set
  • Patterns: tiles, fabrics, lattices
  • Signature props: plants, lanterns, banners, curtains
  • The sign: name, letters, neon colour

Open question. Our town has only its own fictional history so far. Can a business be "Mexican-inspired" or "Japanese-inspired", or should we invent fictional regional cuisines that echo real ones? The shop names here are fictional either way, and the themes take ideas from crafts (tiles, textiles, lanterns), never caricatures.

My review of round 2

People are a different league

Real bodies, layered clothes, faces with character, four distinct people you can tell apart by silhouette. They hold up from every side.

The street has a story

Three restaurants that are clearly different places and still one town. The theme system works as a design rule, not just in this picture.

Hero props are ship-ready in style

The stove and the shop fronts carry the small things that make a place lived in: ticks on dials, a towel, a menu board, a room behind the glass.

Still not finished

The people stand stiffly and are not rigged or animated yet. Shoes are chunky. From the side, the apron bib stands off the chest. Shop interiors are simple, and the night shots are darker than a game can be.

Honest ceiling

This is a good stylised indie look, built entirely in code. Animation is the next big risk: these bodies are rig-ready, but walking and cooking motions need a separate spec and a test.
